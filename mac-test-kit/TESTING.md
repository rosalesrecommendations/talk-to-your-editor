# Testing Talk to Your Editor

This is your personal test pass for the simple build. Start on the Mac with the built-in sample, then try a real runner job, then use your phone.

There are no wrong reactions here. If something feels confusing, slow, fragile, or just not like how you think, that is the feedback we need.

## What you are testing

Quick map: the runner is part 1. It makes the first edit and gives you a job folder. This app is part 2: you watch that edit here and direct the fixes.

This version is built around one idea: keep the front door simple. Two choices, both on unless you turn them off. One button. The transcript itself explains every change, and the app learns your style one yes at a time.

### 0. New in 0.16: edit the timeline by hand

This version adds the timeline editing you asked for: when the automatic edit is a little too short or a little too long somewhere, you can fix that spot yourself, the way you would in Descript. Work through these on the IMG_2516 video or a new one.

1. Split a piece: play or pause where a piece should become two, then click Split at playhead (under the timeline) or press Cmd+Shift+S. The piece under the playhead becomes two pieces, and nothing you hear changes.
2. Drag the edges: every piece has a grip at each edge. Drag the right edge right to pull back words or breath the edit cut; drag it left to trim more. Drag the left edge the same way. 3. The card reports progress in plain words: how many files, and each file as it arrives. When the folder is in, the page reloads and the whole app, video, transcript, counts, is now running on that job.
4. The app works on **the copy it took in**. **Or start with the built-in sample.** Click **Try the built-in sample** on the Bring in a job card. The sample loads on the spot, no drop needed. It uses the offline mock, so you can learn the flow without spending anything or depending on the network. (If you saved a Meta key on this Mac with an earlier version, the launcher still uses it without asking; see "Your Meta key" below.)

Two more drop shapes to try:

- **Drop a single video file** (an .mp4 or .mov on its own). It stages and plays right in the Bring in a job card, and the card offers **Make the first edit**: the app can now write down the words and make that first edit itself, no runner needed. Step 2b below walks it.
- **Drop a folder that is not a job** (a folder of photos, say). The card says plainly that it is not a finished-edit job, names the two files a job folder carries, and confirms nothing was loaded. Your current job, if you have one, keeps running untouched.

If dragging is awkward, the card's **choose a folder** and **choose files** buttons bring in the same things the picking way.

Things worth noticing while you test this: whether the progress wording keeps up with a big folder, whether the reload lands you where you expect, and whether the staged copy behaving like a launched job feels seamless or surprising anywhere.

### 2b. Or drop one video and make its first edit

New in 0.6.0. A single raw video can become a job by itself.

1. Drop one video file (or pick it with **choose files**). Below it a block appears: the **Remove** choices, a direction box, and the **Make the first edit** button. If the video is one this browser cannot play as-is (an iPhone video, most often), the card first says **Getting your video ready to watch…**: a watchable copy is made in the background, at a size that finishes, and the preview swaps in when it lands. You do not have to wait for the copy to make the first edit. If the copy cannot be made on this Mac, the card says so in plain words; the edit still works.
2. **Set the Remove choices for this video.** One block headed **Remove** holds the three removals: **Retakes** (and which take stays), **Silence** (the buffer, and whether the natural breath is kept), and **Filler words**: "Take out the ums and uhs." The block also asks **How involved do you want to be?**: **Cleanest cut** (the editor finishes everything it is sure about, and the spots it is unsure about wait in gold in the transcript) or **Tailor it with me** (every unsure spot is brought to you in gold as a decision). They arrive already set to **your usual**: the choices you used last time, remembered across videos in a small private file in your home folder. Flip any of them for this video and the flip becomes your usual for next time. Changing a choice on the **Make the edit** card (step 3) updates the same usual.
3. **Give direction first, if you have any.** Type it in plain words, or press **Dictate** and speak it (see "Dictating" below). Try: "remove retakes, keep the first clean take." Words that name a control the app has (retakes on or off, which take stays, silence on or off, a buffer like 0.2 seconds, breaths kept or trimmed) set that run's switches, and what you say wins over the saved settings for this video. Anything beyond the switches ("keep it tight and keep the story moving") is read by a model pass over the whole transcript when a Meta key is saved (see step 4); with no key saved, it waits as overall guidance, and the recap says so in one sentence.
4. Press **Make the first edit**. A progress block fills the card: the current stage in plain words, which step of how many, and a moving bar. The stages: listening to your video, writing the words down, measuring the silences from the sound itself, laying out the first plan, checking for retakes, cutting the silence, building your review video.
   - **First run on this Mac, and it can take a while:** with no ElevenLabs key saved, the words are written down on this Mac by a speech model the app downloads the first time (about 150 MB). The progress block says so. Later runs skip the download; the writing-down itself still takes some minutes for a long video on a laptop, and a video of 5 minutes or more says to expect that.
   - **A video with no speech** (music only, say) ends with a plain "no words were found" note and no job. That is the honest answer, not an error.
5. When it lands, a short recap names what the direction set, how many retakes came out and how many were set aside for your call, how many filler words came out, and how many silence joins were trimmed. When a Meta key is saved and your direction said more than the switches hold, the recap adds what the direction pass made of it ("Read your direction and made N changes"), and any **Also noticed** lines: a point made again, a thread never returned to, a strong line buried in flatter material. Those flags never cut anything; they are there for your call. The recap also prices the run (new in 0.7.2, step 10 below): one line per feature that ran, each an estimate, and a total. A few seconds later the page reloads on the new job: video, transcript, dots, counts, exactly like a runner job. Steps 3 to 7 below all apply from here.
6. **Captions:** under **Revised edit**, a link downloads the caption file, built from the words that stayed in the edit, on the edit's own timing. Beside it, the **Hide ums and uhs in captions** switch (on unless you turn it off) leaves the fillers (um, uh, and friends) out of the caption text only. Your video's sound and the written-down words keep everything. Flipping the switch rebuilds the caption file, or removes it, right away. The same switch sits on the **Finished edit** card (step 7) when it appears.

**Dictating.** Every feedback box now has a **Dictate** button: the front-door direction box, a pinned comment, the overall feedback box, the note on an edit, and the direction box above. Press it and the button turns into a red recording indicator counting the seconds while you speak; press it again to stop, and the button and its box show **Writing your words…** until Meta's voice transcription lands your words in the box. Read them over and fix anything before you save, because the box is what counts. With no Meta key saved on this Mac and none pasted on the page, the button answers with one plain sentence saying dictation needs the Meta sign-in, and typing carries on as always. In the note on an edit, a second button, **Use the browser's voice typing instead**, appears when your browser offers it; it is the fallback, never the default.

### 3. Make the edit: the front door

Under the video is the card that matters most: **Make the edit**.

Three choices sit there under the heading **Remove**, all on:

- **Remove retakes.** When a line is said more than once, the earlier tries come out. The app now measures this itself, on your own computer, before any model is asked: clear retakes (the same line said again right after, the later take complete) are taken out through the same safety checks as every other change, even when you have pinned no comments at all. The take that completes its sentence cleanly beats a broken or trailing one; among complete takes, the last one stays.
- **Cut silence.** Long silences are trimmed down toward the buffer.
- **Take out the ums and uhs.** The filler words come out of the sound itself. The list is fixed and conservative (um, uh, er, ah, hmm, and their stretched spellings), the same list the captions use; protected moments are never touched, and every filler removed lands as a transcript dot you can bring back.

Pairs that are close but not clear are judged by the model in one call, flow first: which delivery finishes the sentence, which one carries the story forward, and whether keeping both would pull attention away from it. Pairs carrying a "not", and short lines said twice that could be emphasis, are never cut by the machine; they are left in the transcript for you to decide, with the reason in plain words. With no model available, the near pairs become flags with the match explained, never silence. A line is never cut just for failing to serve the direction; story questions become flags, not cuts.

The **silence buffer** is how much silence stays between one word and the next. Slide it or type the number. It starts at **0.1 seconds**. Cutting silence only ever trims; it never adds quiet back, and it goes through the same safety checks as every other change.

Below the removals, one more choice: **How involved do you want to be?**

- **Cleanest cut** (the default). The editor finishes everything it is sure about. The spots it is unsure about wait in gold in the transcript, for whenever you want them. One kind of spot settles itself here: when your direction asks for a cut and the words it removes say "not" (a negative can flip a meaning, so the editor is careful with them), the cut still goes through when the passage is a repeat of words that stay, or when your direction named those very words. A copy coming out cannot change what the kept copy says.
- **Tailor it with me.** Every spot the editor is unsure about, including every one of those negative-statement cuts, is presented to you in gold as a decision, before it counts.

The choice is remembered in your usual like the others, and every recap names the way of working the run used, and how many spots are waiting in gold.

Press **Make the edit**. That is the whole front door. The app checks retakes, takes out the fillers, trims the silences, and tells you the result: words kept before and after, how many retakes came out, how many were left for you to decide, how many filler words came out, and how many pauses were trimmed.

Behind the **More choices** fold, for the days you want them:

- **Format scope:** both formats, vertical only, or landscape only.
- **When a line is said more than once, keep:** three choices, and the one you pick is remembered.
  - **The last take.** The usual rule: among complete takes, the last one stays.
  - **The first clean take.** The first complete delivery stays and the later tries come out, through the same safety checks.
  - **Best delivery (Beta).** The last take still stays for now. In the background, Beta records the take it would have kept on every pair where there is a genuine choice, using the same flow question the model judges near pairs with: which delivery finishes the sentence and carries the story forward. It changes nothing. Its pick shows as a note on the removed words and in the review rows, and the card keeps its score in plain words: how often its pick matched the take you ended up keeping, once you grade or swap. When two deliveries say the very same words, Beta always calls it a tie; there is nothing in the words to choose between them, so the last take stays. Nothing promotes Beta on its own; the count is there so you can watch it earn the job.
- **Breaths:** what happens to the breath at the start of a sentence when a pause is trimmed.
  - **Keep the natural breath** (the default). A join is never trimmed below the natural breath. A join beside a protected moment, or one a pinned comment points at, is never trimmed under either choice: intention is hard to judge, so those are yours.
  - **Trim breaths with the pause.** The buffer alone rules, and the breath goes with the pause, for the places where the next thought should land right away.
  - Either way, every trimmed join lands as a transcript dot with its reason and can be brought back, and the recap names the state in plain words.
- **Spoken direction:** anything else this video needs, in your own words. What you say adds to the choices; it does not replace them. Say **"unless"** when you want one spot treated differently, like "keep the last take, unless the first try has the real laugh in it." Breaths can be set by voice too: "keep the breaths natural" or "cut the breaths too."

Your choices are remembered as your usual, in a small private file in your home folder, so the card opens the way you left it on every video, and the drop block at step 2b starts from the same usual.

### 4. Watch and scrub

Play the edit. A few things changed about moving through it:

- **Drag the timeline** and the handle grows under your finger or cursor. A bubble above it shows the exact time where you will land when you let go.
- **Back 5s** and **Back 1s** buttons sit beside the player for quick steps back.
- The keyboard still works the way the big editors do, as long as you are not typing in a box: **Space** plays or pauses, **K** pauses, **J** steps back 5 seconds, **L** steps forward 5 seconds, the **arrow keys** step 1 second, **Shift** with an arrow steps 5 seconds, and **M** starts a note pinned right at the playhead. The quiet line under the video lists them all, and its switch turns them off for the session.

### 5. Pin comments, when a spot needs your words

The front door handles the patterns. For a specific moment, pin a comment:

1. When you hear something you want changed, just start typing in the comment box. The note pins itself to wherever the playhead is at that moment. Typing pauses the video; switch on **Keep playing while I type** if you would rather keep listening.
2. **The time does not have to be exact.** A pinned time is an estimate. The editor checks a few seconds either way, about three seconds, and resolves your note to the nearest words by context. Get close; the app does the rest.
3. Write the kind of thing you would naturally say, such as "Remove this repeated line" or "Bring back the first version of this sentence."
4. Choose where the comment applies. It starts at **Both**, meaning both the vertical and the landscape cut.
5. Save the comment. Each new comment pins fresh at its own moment.

For a note about the whole video rather than one moment, like "there are retakes all through this," use the **Overall feedback** box. Overall notes go along with every direction run.

Then either press **Make the edit** on the front door, or use the **Run direction** card lower down, which is the same direction step with the provider picker showing.

Each change comes back with a clear status:

- **Applied:** the program made the change.
- **Flagged:** the program did not change it. A person needs to decide, often because the note touches protected material, a special breath or insert, an unclear target, or wording that could change the meaning.
- **Rejected:** the proposed change failed a check and was not applied.

The reason is always in plain language. If a status surprises you, that surprise is useful test data.

### 6. Read the transcript: every removal explains itself

Open the **Transcript** section. The words live in their own scrolling panel now, grouped into lines, each line opening with a small time chip. Kept words read normally; cut words are struck through and dimmed.

- **Following the video.** While the video plays, the line and word you are hearing light up, and the panel keeps them in view: that is **Follow the video**, the switch at the panel's head, on unless you turn it off. Scroll the panel yourself and following hands control back to you until you press play again. Tap a line's time chip to jump the video to that line.
- **Picking words.** Click a word and the video jumps to it and picks it (Shift and click picks a stretch). A strip pinned at the panel's foot offers **Cut these words**, **Bring these words back**, and **Fix the words** for the pick, through the same safety checks as every other change.
- **The dots.** Every span this app has taken out carries a small **dot** after it. Rest on it (Mac) or tap it (phone) and a small card opens beside the dot, never covering the words it explains:
  - The card names the words that were removed and **why**, in one plain sentence, with the note that asked for it when there is one.
  - When the removed words were a retake, the card also shows **the take that stayed**, and two buttons: **Bring these words back** and **Swap takes**. Swap takes puts the removed take back and takes the stayed one out instead, in one checked step.
  - You can **leave a note on that edit** right there. Type it, or dictate it. The note is stored against the edit itself, not against a timestamp, so it keeps meaning the same thing even after the timing shifts. It is listed under that edit from then on, and the next direction run receives it labeled with what was removed. No note is ever lost.
  - Esc closes the card; scrolling the panel closes it too.
- **The amber markers.** An amber flag marker is an **Also noticed** flag from the direction pass (step 2b): something the app noticed that you did not ask about. Its card quotes the words and says the notice in one sentence. Flags never removed anything; there is nothing to bring back, only something to see.
- **The gold spots.** Some words sit washed **gold**, with a small gold marker after them. Those are the spots a pass suspected but did not settle: a retake it measured but could not cut on its own, or a change it held back (often because the words include a negative statement, and changing a negative can change the meaning). Rest on the marker (Mac) or tap it (phone) and its card says what the spot is and why the editor is unsure, and asks for your call: **Cut it** or **Leave it**. A cut goes through the same safety checks as every other change, right then. Leaving a spot settles it: it is never offered again in this job. You can add a note before you decide, typed or dictated; the note is saved with your verdict, and your verdicts teach the app the same way your grades do (step 8): call the same kind of spot the same way a few times and the app will offer it as a rule in your words, with your note as the context.

**Fix words** still repairs words the transcript heard wrong, from the word strip or the button under the panel. Fixing words changes the transcript text only; the sound and the timing never change.

### 7. Build the fixed video, then grade the changes

Rendering sets itself up when it can. If the job folder already carries the pieces rendering needs (the render script, the cleaned audio, and the original video), the app finds and uses them on its own. If a piece is missing, the page says which piece, in one plain sentence.

1. Press **Build the fixed video**.
2. Press **Grade the changes** to open the review page.
3. Watch the changed moments and grade each one: **Worked**, **Partly**, or **Did not work**. Retakes get their own rows, showing both takes. Grading a retake **Did not work** retires that pair: it is never proposed again.
4. On any change that is simply how you edit, check **This is my style**.
5. Press **Send grades to the app**. Your grades go straight back into the app. (The export button still works too; keep the file if you want a copy.)

**The finish card.** Once the fixed video is built and you have opened the review page, a **Finished edit** card appears on the main page: the latest video you built from this edit, ready to watch or to download as finished-edit.mp4, and for a job the app took in itself, the caption file beside it, with the same **Hide ums and uhs in captions** switch. If the card is not there yet, one of those two halves has not happened; it never claims a finish the app cannot see. If a job folder is ever missing a file it needs, the page says so in one plain sentence and keeps the job's actions out of sight until the folder is whole again; you will never land on a screen of raw code.

### 8. Your style: rules you approve

Back on the main page, the **Your style** card is where the learning lands.

When the same kind of change keeps working in your grades, three times, or once when you marked it "This is my style," the app offers it once, in plain words:

> Pattern caught: (the pattern, in your words). Make this a rule for future edits?

- **Save as a rule** and it rides into every later run as standing direction, alongside your own notes.
- **Not now** and it waits. If the pattern keeps earning grades, it can be offered again later.
- Every saved rule is listed with a **Remove** button. Removing one is always one tap.

Nothing is ever saved without your yes. There is no silent learning.

### 9. Undo, redo, and History

At the bottom of the page is a **History** card. Every change in this session is recorded there: comments saved, front door runs, words cut or restored, notes on edits, grades, and rules.

- Press **Undo** to step back one change, or **Redo** to step forward again.
- Stepping back never touches the runner job itself; it only moves your session.

Try it once: make a small change you do not care about, undo it, then redo it. The page, the transcript, and the results should all follow.

### 10. What it costs

New in 0.7.2. The money is on the page, before and after every run.

1. **Before you press the button.** Under **Make the first edit** (at the drop) and under **Make the edit**, one quiet line estimates the run: "This first edit usually costs about X." Change the Remove choices or write direction and the line follows. The estimate comes from the video's length, your choices, and what your recent runs on this Mac actually cost; on a Mac with no run history yet, it prices from the rates built into the app.
2. **After a run.** The first edit's recap, and the result lines under **Make the edit**, carry one line per feature that ran, with its estimated cost, plus a total. A feature that did not run does not appear. Every estimate carries the word "about": the model rates are derived from our one real bill, not published, and the app says so by how it words them.
3. **The money card.** A card titled **What the editing has cost** shows this job's costs and the running total on this Mac, broken down by feature. It appears once anything is on the books. **Reset the running total** starts the Mac's total over; each job's own record stays in its folder. The same per-job lines sit on the **Finished edit** card when it appears.
4. **What to expect.** The work that runs on this Mac is free and reads "0 cents": the words written down locally, the ums and uhs, the silence. Writing the words down with ElevenLabs Scribe runs about 4 cents for a 10-minute video. The retake check is about 2 cents, reading your direction about 5. The one big line is **Second review (Muse Wonder)**, roughly 59 cents on a run whose direction needs it: Wonder's replies are the cost driver, exactly as the real bill showed. Dictated notes are on the books too, at a fraction of a cent each. If a number ever looks wrong to you, that is the feedback we need.

### Your Meta key

Live Muse direction uses your Meta Model API key. The launcher never asks for it anymore.

- **A key saved by an earlier version still works.** If you once answered yes to remembering a key on this Mac, it sits in `~/.talk-to-your-editor/meta_key` (folder and file readable only by your account), and the launcher uses it without asking. It never enters the app folder, the kit, the zip, or the project files. Delete that one file and the app goes back to the offline mock at launch.
- **Save on this Mac (new in 0.6.1).** On the page, each key has a **Save on this Mac** button next to **Use this key for this session**: the Meta key, and an ElevenLabs key (for writing down dropped videos) with its own field. Saving writes the same private file the launcher reads, so the key survives restarts and new kit downloads; the card then shows the key is saved on this Mac, with a **Remove** option beside it. Saving an ElevenLabs key the same way lands in `~/.talk-to-your-editor/elevenlabs_key`.
- **On the page:** the Meta sign-in card (the Run direction card once a job is loaded) shows whether a sign-in is set. Paste a key there and use it for the session only, and it is kept in memory, never written to disk, and never shown back. Press **Check the Meta sign-in** before a live run; it makes one harmless call and tells you in plain words whether the sign-in was accepted.

Never paste the key into chat. The page's sign-in field is the place it belongs.

## Test with a real runner job

Open the app, then drop the real job folder onto the page (see "The app opens empty. Drop the folder." above). The app reloads on the dropped job and you are testing it. That is the whole route.

The older route still works for anyone who wants it: drag the job folder onto the **launcher file itself** in Finder before it opens, and the app starts on that job directly. You never have to do this; dropping on the page lands in the same place.

### Which folder to drop

Drop the **runner job or export folder itself**, not a single file inside it.

The right folder contains `transcript.json` and `cuts.json` together. It may also contain `profile.json`, `feedback.json`, `review.mp4` or `final.mp4`, and other files from the runner export. If you only see the video, or a folder full of other job folders, you are one level too high or too low. Drop the wrong thing and the page says so, plainly, and loads nothing.

### Real-job pass

1. Drop the job folder on the page.
2. Press **Make the edit** with the two choices on and watch what the front door does on its own.
3. Pin a comment or two in your own words where the front door did not cover it. Do not aim for exact times.
4. Run it again and check every status and reason.
5. Open the transcript, click a dot, and leave a note on one edit.
6. Build the fixed video, grade the changes, mark one "This is my style" if it earns it, and send the grades to the app.
7. Check the **Your style** card for the rule it offers.

For a live run, repeat the same job later with the key. Comparing mock and live on the same comments makes differences easy to spot.

## Phone test

The phone uses the app running on your Mac. The Mac does the work; the phone is another screen for the same test.

1. Keep the Mac awake, and keep the Terminal window open.
2. Connect the phone and the Mac to the **same Wi-Fi**.
3. On the phone, open the phone address printed by the launcher. It will look like this:

```text
http://192.168.1.25:8765
```

Your numbers will be different. Do not use the `127.0.0.1` address on the phone; that address points the phone back at itself.

4. Play the edit on the phone.
5. Scrub with a finger: the handle should grow, and the bubble should show where you will land.
6. Pin a comment from the phone. For a note on an edit, tap a dot in the transcript and dictate with the keyboard's microphone.
7. Press **Make the edit**, then open **Grade the changes** and grade one change on the phone.
8. Note anything that is hard to tap, hard to read, slow, or awkward with one hand.

If the phone cannot load the page, first check that both devices are on the same Wi-Fi and that the Mac did not fall asleep. Then restart the launcher so it prints a fresh phone address.

## Stop the app

Close the Terminal window, or click into it and press **Control-C**.

To test again, double-click the launcher again. The app opens empty each time; drop the job folder, or click the built-in sample.

## What to report back

Use your own words. Short is fine. Voice notes are fine.

Please send:

- What you were trying to do.
- What happened instead.
- The comment you pinned, if a comment was involved.
- The status and reason the app gave.
- Whether the revised edit sounded right.
- Anything that felt confusing, fragile, slow, or unlike your normal way of giving notes.
